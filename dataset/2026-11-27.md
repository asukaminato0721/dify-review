Live precision is right / (right + wrong). Read it as an upper bound:

- `right` almost always means the commented lines changed afterwards. Of the
  326, 325 are "resolved". About 318 of those were outdated (the lines
  changed, then the bot resolved its own thread), and 17 were resolved with
  the lines unchanged, after a human reply. A change to those lines is not
  proof that the author took the suggestion. On large pull requests that keep
  being rewritten, lines change anyway.
- `wrong` is a 👎 or a reply that Jev reads as disagreeing (all 18 are replies).
  Silent disagreement shows up as `ignored` only after the pull request
  closes.
- The bot comments most on the largest pull requests: #45896 (1953 changed
  lines, 23 comments), #46457 (3624 lines, 22 comments), #46460 (19
  comments). From this window on, such pull requests get a split request and
  no Jev review (`--max-pr-lines 1500`).

## New rule: `use-inspect-unwrap`

Six comments asked for `inspect.unwrap`, or for the original function, instead
of a hand-written `unwrap` helper or a hand walk over `__wrapped__`. The rule
is gated on a hint (`__wrapped__|\bdef\s+_?unwrap\w*\s*[\[(]`, checked on each
instance, including PEP 695 `def unwrap[T](`). Backtest: 4/6 hits with no
noise at 0.30, and 2/6 at the 0.80 it was created with. The two misses (Jev
0.28) are bare "rm" comments on `@overload` stubs of the helper, where the
question has the least to go on.
